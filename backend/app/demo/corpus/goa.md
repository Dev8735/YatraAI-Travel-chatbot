# Overview
Goa is India's smallest state by area, on the western coast. It was a Portuguese colony until 1961, and Portuguese influence remains visible in its churches, architecture and cuisine. The capital is Panaji (Panjim).

# Beaches: North vs South
North Goa beaches such as Baga, Calangute and Anjuna are lively, with more nightlife, water sports and crowds. South Goa beaches such as Palolem and Agonda are generally quieter and better suited to a relaxed stay. Many travellers split their stay between the two.

# Heritage
The Churches and Convents of Old Goa are a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 1986). The Basilica of Bom Jesus holds the relics of St. Francis Xavier, and the Se Cathedral is one of the largest churches in Asia. In Panaji, the Fontainhas Latin Quarter has colourful Portuguese-era houses and narrow lanes that are pleasant to explore on foot. Fort Aguada, a 17th-century Portuguese fort with a lighthouse, overlooks the sea near Candolim.

# Nature
Dudhsagar Falls, on the Goa–Karnataka border, is a tall, multi-tiered waterfall that is most impressive after the monsoon. Access and jeep safaris are often restricted during heavy rains, so check the current status.

# Seasons
November to February is peak season in Goa, with dry, pleasant weather and the highest prices. The monsoon, roughly June to September, brings heavy rain and rough seas; swimming is unsafe and many beach shacks close, but the landscape turns green and prices drop.

# Getting around
Rented scooters and taxis are the common ways to get around Goa. Carry a valid driving licence, wear a helmet, and avoid driving after drinking. Goan dishes to try include fish curry rice, xacuti, vindaloo and the layered dessert bebinca.

# Practical tips
Three to five days suits a first visit to Goa. Respect lifeguard flags at beaches, and keep valuables secure.
