# Overview
Kerala, on India's south-western coast, combines beaches, tea-covered hills and a network of backwater lagoons and canals. A classic first trip links Kochi, Munnar and Alleppey (Alappuzha) over five to seven days.

# Kochi
Fort Kochi is known for its cantilevered Chinese fishing nets, colonial-era streets and cafés. St. Francis Church is one of the oldest European churches in India; Vasco da Gama was originally buried there before his remains were moved to Portugal. Mattancherry Palace (also called the Dutch Palace) has notable Kerala murals.

The Paradesi Synagogue, built in 1568, is in the Jew Town area of Kochi; it is usually closed on Saturdays and Jewish holidays, so check opening days. Short Kathakali performances for visitors, including the make-up session, are held in Fort Kochi in the evenings. In biennale years the Kochi-Muziris Biennale contemporary art festival usually runs from December to March.

# Munnar
Munnar is a hill station in the Western Ghats, surrounded by tea estates. Eravikulam National Park protects the endangered Nilgiri tahr; the park is usually closed for a period around February to April during the tahr calving season. The Neelakurinji shrub blooms only once every twelve years — the last mass bloom was in 2018. A tea museum explains the history of tea processing in the region. Roads to Munnar are winding, so allow extra time.

# Alleppey backwaters
Alleppey (Alappuzha) is the main base for Kerala backwater trips. Traditional houseboats (kettuvallam) offer day cruises or overnight stays; government-approved houseboats are recommended. The Nehru Trophy snake boat race is held on Punnamada Lake, usually on the second Saturday of August.

# Seasons
The south-west monsoon usually reaches Kerala around the start of June and brings heavy rain until about September. Many people visit Kerala from September to March. The monsoon is also a traditional season for Ayurvedic treatments.
