# Overview
Jaipur is the capital of Rajasthan and is often called the Pink City because buildings in the old walled city were painted a terracotta pink in 1876. The city was founded in 1727 by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II and was laid out on a planned grid. The walled city of Jaipur was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019.

# Key sights
Amber Fort (also spelled Amer Fort) sits on a hill about 11 km north of the old city and is part of the UNESCO-listed Hill Forts of Rajasthan. Plan at least two to three hours there, and go early in the morning to avoid crowds and midday heat.

Hawa Mahal, the "Palace of Winds", was built in 1799 by Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh. Its honeycomb facade of small windows let royal women watch street life without being seen. The front facade is best photographed in the morning light.

The City Palace complex is still partly a royal residence and houses museum galleries of textiles, arms and paintings. Next door, Jantar Mantar is an 18th-century astronomical observatory with large masonry instruments; it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010.

Nahargarh Fort overlooks the city from the Aravalli ridge and is popular at sunset. Albert Hall Museum, the oldest museum in Rajasthan, is lit up in the evening.

# Food and shopping
Classic Rajasthani dishes to try include dal baati churma, gatte ki sabzi, pyaaz kachori and the sweet ghewar. Johari Bazaar is known for jewellery and Bapu Bazaar for textiles and mojari footwear. The nearby villages of Sanganer and Bagru are known for traditional hand block printing.

# Practical tips
The most comfortable time to visit Jaipur is October to March; April to June is very hot. A composite ticket covering several state-run monuments has been offered by Rajasthan's tourism department — check the official site for current validity and price. Bargaining is normal in bazaars. Two to three days covers the main sights; add a day for Amber and Nahargarh at a relaxed pace.
