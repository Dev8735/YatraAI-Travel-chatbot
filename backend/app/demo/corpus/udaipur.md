# Overview
Udaipur, in southern Rajasthan, is known as the City of Lakes. It was founded in 1559 by Maharana Udai Singh II as the new capital of the Mewar kingdom. The old city is built around Lake Pichola and framed by the Aravalli hills.

# Key sights
The City Palace stands on the eastern bank of Lake Pichola. It is a large complex of courtyards, balconies and towers, and much of it is now a museum. Allow about two hours.

Boat rides on Lake Pichola pass Jag Mandir island and the Lake Palace (Jag Niwas), which is now a hotel. Water levels depend on the previous monsoon, so boating can be limited after a weak rainy season.

Jagdish Temple, near the City Palace, is a 17th-century temple dedicated to Vishnu. Bagore Ki Haveli on Gangaur Ghat is a museum by day and hosts an evening folk dance and puppet show; check the current show time locally.

Saheliyon Ki Bari is an ornamental garden with fountains. Fateh Sagar Lake, north of Pichola, has a lakeside promenade. The Monsoon Palace (Sajjangarh) sits on a hilltop west of the city and is a popular sunset viewpoint.

# Day trips
Kumbhalgarh Fort, known for its very long fortified walls, and the Jain temple at Ranakpur are often combined in a long day trip from Udaipur. Chittorgarh Fort is another option, about three hours away by road.

# Practical tips
October to March is the most comfortable season to visit Udaipur. The old-city lanes are narrow, so walking or auto-rickshaws work better than cars. Two to three days suits a relaxed visit, plus a day for an excursion.
