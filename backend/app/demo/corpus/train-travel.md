# Booking train tickets
Reserved Indian Railways tickets are booked online through IRCTC (the website and the IRCTC Rail Connect app) or at railway reservation counters. Online booking requires an IRCTC account.

The advance reservation period — how far ahead you can book a train ticket — has changed over time. It was reduced from 120 days to 60 days (excluding the journey date) from 1 November 2024. Always check IRCTC for the current rule.

Tatkal tickets are a last-minute quota that opens one day before the journey (excluding the journey date): typically at 10:00 for AC classes and 11:00 for non-AC classes. Tatkal fares are higher and tickets sell out quickly.

# Train classes
Common classes include Sleeper (SL), AC 3-tier (3A), AC 2-tier (2A), AC First Class (1A), AC Chair Car (CC) and Executive Chair Car (EC). Chair car classes are used on day trains such as Shatabdi and Vande Bharat services.

# Waitlists and PNR
A ticket may be Confirmed (CNF), RAC (Reservation Against Cancellation — you can board and share a berth) or Waitlisted (WL). Each booking has a 10-digit PNR number used to track its status. Fully waitlisted e-tickets are usually cancelled automatically if they do not get confirmed.

# Checking schedules
Use NTES (National Train Enquiry System) or the official IRCTC and Indian Railways sites for train schedules and running status. Third-party apps can be convenient but may lag behind official data.

# Tips
Carry an original photo ID matching the ticket. Avoid touts at stations. Long-distance trains can run late, especially in winter fog in north India, so leave buffers before connecting flights or trains.
