# Ahmedabad
The historic walled city of Ahmedabad became India's first UNESCO World Heritage City in 2017. Its old quarters are organised into pols — dense neighbourhoods of carved wooden houses — which are best seen on a guided heritage walk in the morning.

Sabarmati Ashram was Mahatma Gandhi's home from 1917 to 1930, and the Dandi (Salt) March began here in 1930. Sidi Saiyyed Mosque is famous for its intricate stone jali (lattice) window showing an intertwined tree. Adalaj Stepwell, about 18 km north of Ahmedabad, is a richly carved five-storey stepwell built in 1499. The Calico Museum of Textiles has an important textile collection but can only be visited on limited guided tours that must be booked in advance.

Manek Chowk becomes a busy street-food market at night. Try Gujarati dishes such as dhokla, khandvi, fafda-jalebi, and a full Gujarati thali.

# Rann of Kutch
The Great Rann of Kutch is a vast seasonal salt desert that turns into a white expanse in the dry months. The Rann Utsav festival, with a tent city at Dhordo, is usually held from about November to February. Some areas close to the international border require a permit, which can be arranged locally or online. Bhuj is the main town and a base for visiting craft villages known for embroidery and block printing.

# Gir National Park
Gir National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary, around Sasan Gir, is the only place in the world where Asiatic lions live in the wild. Jeep safari permits are booked through the official Gir booking portal, and numbers are limited. The park is closed to safaris during the monsoon, from mid-June to mid-October.

# Saurashtra coast and pilgrimages
Somnath Temple, on the Gujarat coast, is revered as the first of the twelve jyotirlingas. Dwarka, at the western tip of the peninsula, is home to the Dwarkadhish Temple and is an important Krishna pilgrimage site.

# Statue of Unity
The Statue of Unity at Ekta Nagar (Kevadia) depicts Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and, at 182 metres, is the tallest statue in the world. It stands near the Sardar Sarovar Dam on the Narmada. Tickets for the viewing gallery are booked online; the site is typically closed on Mondays — check before visiting.

# Practical notes
Gujarat has prohibition laws: alcohol is not sold freely, although visitors can apply for a liquor permit. The Navratri festival (September/October) is celebrated with nightly garba dancing across the state. October to March is the most comfortable season to visit Gujarat.
